## What we looked for and did not find

- **Street-light locations.** No official open lamp-column dataset exists for Westminster or Kensington & Chelsea
  (checked: data.gov.uk publisher listings for both councils and TfL, all 1,310 London Datastore titles, Westminster's
  street-lighting page, TfL's open data catalogue). Some other boroughs do publish one (Camden, Barnet, Lambeth).
  OSM `highway=street_lamp` has only 148 nodes in the area, too sparse to use. The OSM `lit` tag is therefore the
  only lighting signal, and its gaps are treated as unknown.
- **Pedestrian counts on streets.** None openly available. TfL publishes station-level data only
  (`https://api.tfl.gov.uk/crowding/{naptan}` gives a relative 15-minute profile; daily tap counts in
  `StationFootfall_2025_2026 .csv`). The GLA High Streets footfall data is restricted to subscribed boroughs.
  We did not turn station busyness into a street-level signal.

## Sources verified but deliberately not used in the route score

- **Recorded crime** (police.uk `crimes-street` API, OGL v3): monthly, no time of day, and every crime is moved to
  the nearest of a fixed list of anonymous map points. It cannot distinguish night from day or one side of a
  junction from another, and without pedestrian exposure a count is not a rate.
- **Road casualties** (DfT STATS19, OGL v3): exact coordinates, time and `light_conditions`, so it is the one
  outcome dataset that could support a fitted night-time model. We did not fit one in 24 hours because the
  denominator (how many people walk each segment at night) is not available, and a count per segment mostly
  measures how busy the segment is.

Both are documented here so the omission is a visible decision, not an oversight. See `docs/MODEL.md`.
